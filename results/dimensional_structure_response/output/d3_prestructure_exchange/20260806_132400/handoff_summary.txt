DSD D3 prestructure-exchange and axial-tension simulation handoff
Author: Kwon Dominicus
Status: Construction / Consistency Check / Exploratory simulation

Scope
- base universe dimension is fixed at D3 throughout
- channels 4, 5, and 6 are N-dimensional prestructure exchange pathways projected onto D3 axes
- they are not realized additional dimensions
- default direct high-rank-to-particle coupling is zero

Baseline results
- balanced isotropic exchange: 2.962222 e-fold; first channel isolation 1.734 tau; particle isolation 2.386 tau
- anisotropic single-channel exchange: peak tension deviation 0.129374; 2.651897 e-fold despite greater inflow than the balanced case
- outflow-dominant case: convergence begins at 12.704 tau; final H=-0.038557
- rapid expansion/isolation: 4.835764 e-fold; channel isolation 1.202 tau; particle isolation 1.708 tau

Key finding
- high-rank energy can strongly alter D3 metric response and connectivity while direct particle-energy import remains exactly zero
- symmetric inflow favors isotropic expansion
- anisotropic inflow remains partly stored as D3 axial-tension deviation
- outflow produces delayed convergence through a response-memory term
- cross channels isolate before the particle population in all four baseline scenarios

125-cell grid
- expansion/isolation D3: 97
- converging D3: 19
- scale-floor contracted D3: 9
- final connected cross channels: 0 in 112 cells; 1 in 4; 2 in 2; 3 in 7
- maximum e-fold: 4.993932

Numerical audit
- deterministic rerun matched five core CSV files byte-for-byte
- maximum baseline energy-ledger error <= 5.37e-13

Interpretive limit
- expansion equation is phenomenological, not a Friedmann derivation
- indirect particle response is a response proxy, not an observable energy
- scale-floor contraction is a numerical stress outcome, not D3 extinction
